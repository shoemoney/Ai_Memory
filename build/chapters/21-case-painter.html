The business being replaced

“What colour and sheen did we use in the Hendersons' dining room?” The assistant answers with a signed completion-card link. Cedar Brush Painting is invented: one owner, an occasional helper. The assistant retrieves house-specific evidence and drafts actions. Owner-controlled hardware runs every model; no hosted model API receives prompts, photographs or customer memory.

SQLite (Chapter 12) holds room colours, estimate lines and payments. Chapter 20's bounded memory-to-prompt loop runs here with separate code, invented data and the 2B on a Linux CPU. The recommended 9B, vision, voice and Mac paths are unrun. This engineer's build specification is no tested accounting package or practitioner deployment.

At a site visit, the owner records rooms, surface areas, prep, access and photographs. An estimate specifies surfaces, coats, product, sheen, materials, labour and exclusions. Acceptance triggers a deposit request and a job date; added trim or repairs need a separately approved change order. Completion photos and leftover-paint notes lead to a final invoice, payment, review request, warranty record and repaint date. The paid tools sell the same steps: PaintScout lists production rates and materials lists, Joist lists photos and change orders.12

External boundaries remain: a processor handles card payments and confirms settlement; a provider delivers messages; the accountant supplies sales-tax rules. Adapters send only approved amounts, contacts and message text. This reference design supplies no legal, compliance or tax advice.

What this leaves out

  • A full CRM. Its administration exceeds the owner's need for dependable tools.
  • A voice receptionist. Truck notes need transcription, not live telephony.
  • Autonomous estimating from photos. An image gives no reliable dimensions or prep difficulty.

Compare one chosen subscription with annual hardware depreciation, electricity and maintenance hours at the owner's rate. Never stack alternative subscriptions' savings. Payment fees and setup reduce savings; maintenance may exceed Joist's price. Data ownership and property recall justify the design.

Architecture decisions, November 2026

Hardware and local models

Start with owner-controlled hardware. Apple lists a candidate Mac mini M6 with 32 GB unified memory and 1 TB storage. CPU and GPU share memory; 16 GB leaves little room for model, context, application, uploads and another model.5 The 32 GB price is unverified. Get a quote; run acceptance gates on borrowed or returnable hardware before purchase. Any passing machine suffices.

Use Qwen3.5-9B as the main local assistant, served by Ollama 0.40.16. Its card lists 9 billion language-model parameters, Apache-2.0 and 262,144 native tokens; the Ollama package adds a vision projector and is labelled for tool support. The qwen3.5:9b download is about 6.6 GB with its vision part, and download size is not runtime memory (Chapter 06 covers the packaging).67

Set 8,192 working tokens. Budget roughly 12 to 16 GB for package, context cache, embedder and headroom; this is unmeasured. Queue vision and transcription behind chat; verify peak memory at installation.

The 9B card defaults to thinking, so send think: false to Ollama for record extraction and routine drafts, and check that the installed package returns no thinking trace (Chapter 06). If a trace appears, treat the package as mislabelled and pin a different build. Ollama supplies tool-call messages and JSON-schema constraints, but neither makes an argument true or a write authorised.89

Qwen3.5-2B Q8_0 uses 8-bit weights; Q4 uses smaller 4-bit weights (Chapter 06). Normal role: read photos, receipts and can labels at 4,096 tokens. Fallback role: after a 9B load failure or speed regression on accepted hardware, reload the 2B at 8,192 tokens (fallback_context in config.json). Suspend vision; render SQL templates until the 2B passes each task's draft gate. When the 9B resumes chat, return the 2B to vision. Its card lists image input, Apache-2.0 and the same native context maximum; thinking defaults off, unlike the 9B. Keep it off. Treat a label transcription as unapproved until the owner compares it with the image: OCR misreads colour codes, and a photograph never fixes a paint colour. The inspected Ollama 2B package is Q8_0 (about 2.0 GB plus a 671 MB projector), not a Q4 build.1011

Give every kind of memory a home

Join on property_id, identifying a customer's tenure at an address: two houses need two IDs; a sale creates a new ID, preventing buyers from inheriting sellers' notes. Attach dining-room completion evidence to its room and job. Gate codes are current access facts; expired warranties are history, never free-repaint promises.

Chapter 00 defines semantic facts, episodic history, procedures and working context. The separate business ledger stores ordinary money and customer rows accessed through tools; models never rebuild balances from memory.

Keep raw voice files and photograph hashes. The owner accepts or corrects locally proposed transcripts, captions and facts. Commit accepted rows and events atomically, marking embeddings stale until refreshed; meanwhile, use FTS5 rather than older vectors.

A background queue embeds approved notes within minutes. Nightly work retries failures, finds duplicates and proposes diary summaries. The nightly job may rebuild derived FTS5/vector indexes, even from empty, using approved notes and the pinned model digest without owner approval. It cannot change a colour, contact, price, tax code, warranty promise, consent flag or payment state.

A replacement names its superseded note. Acceptance atomically publishes the surface revision, closes the old fact interval, writes its replacement and queues its embedding. Test recall/fallback agreement and retained historical evidence. The original event stays as evidence, subject to Chapter 17's erasure policy.

Search two ways: exact words find “HC-172” and invoice IDs, and embeddings find paraphrases such as “the cream room we did two summers ago.” Filter by customer, property and validity in both branches before selecting candidates. Fuse the rankings with reciprocal rank fusion, 1/(60+rank), using each branch's 1-based rank to avoid raw-score dominance (Chapter 05). The smoke test scans ordinary BLOB rows with sqlite-vec cosine functions, without a vector server.14

Photo search embeds approved captions with a small text embedder. Missing/wrong captions hide photos; allow job-thumbnail browsing. Chapter 04 covers direct image embeddings for visual similarity.

Acceptance gates: speed and correctness

Test synthetic prompts at all five input budgets before accepting hardware. Time loading, prompt processing, decoding and the owner's end-to-end wait separately; the speed gate permits 30 seconds to decode a fixed 300-token draft. Before testing, set the longest acceptable Estimate wait and, per task, the fraction of answerable cases whose unedited drafts must be complete and source-supported. Require abstention on flagged conflicts. Score raw drafts, sent messages, template fallbacks and review fallbacks separately on fixed colour/reminder cases, including superseded colours and disputed accounts. A task missing its draft threshold stays template-only. Reject a purchase if the 9B misses the speed gate. Require zero incorrect releases: unsupported amounts, attachments, promises or access disclosures fail installation.

Authoritative rows: fixture and production

Only the append-only trigger, inline price comparison and backup read-back were tested. Test production controls before retiring paid software.

The tested schema.sql permits fractional quantities despite INTEGER affinity. Integer-cent totals require integer units: square feet, quarter-hours or line items. Production needs STRICT tables or typeof checks for quantities/cents; test half-unit rejection and two-quarter-hour acceptance. Approved rules supply whole-line tax_cents, added once after qty*unit_cents in both totals; models never predict tax. Issued lines copy prices/descriptions, immune to estimate edits. The fixture has 11 completed, invoiced jobs; Vale has a sent estimate but no job, invoice or payment. Vale's jobless completion card is simplified; production follows the acceptance workflow.

What production must add to this tested schema

  • Issued invoices are immutable. Corrections use credit notes: separate linked records reducing the original amount owed, requiring an additional invoice kind and balance logic absent from schema.sql.
  • Payments are allocated once. Record deposits against jobs as unallocated receipts. Invoice issue atomically allocates them: payment_allocations stores payment_id, invoice_id and cents; UNIQUE(payment_id) enforces one invoice per payment. Unique provider event IDs prevent duplicate receipts. schema.sql subtracts all positive payments, so refunds there would wrongly reduce debt. Production needs positive kind=refund rows linked by refund_of; balances subtract payments minus refunds. A $100 refund of a $400 deposit leaves $700 due on $1,000; cumulative refunds above $400 fail.
  • A job, its estimate, each change order and the invoice's source lines share one tenure. Enforce it with composite keys or transactional checks, and test that a valid estimate ID from another property is rejected.
  • Store unique request IDs, payload hashes (fingerprints) and payload-bound approvals. Make posting safe to retry after timeouts or double taps: identical ID/hash retries return prior results; changed hashes under an existing ID fail. New IDs require approval and base-revision checks.
  • Production adds append-only surface_revisions with valid_from/valid_to and source IDs, plus the columns schema.sql lacks: tenure end on properties, contact hold and dispute flag on customers, an access class on notes (bound per task), a stale flag on vectors (set on an accepted edit, filtered in the dense branch), job_id on notes and a revision on every mutable table. Both reminder lists require current tenure, no contact hold and no open dispute. Invoice reminders also require an issued invoice, positive balance and due date before today. Repaint reminders require repaint_on on or before today and marketing consent. Exclude a successful reminder for that surface and repaint_on; recheck before dispatch and test that rerunning sends no duplicate.

Backups protect future reads and the books

Before deployment, pin a backup tool's version; keep its recovery secret off-machine. Run this every night at 02:00:

  1. Complete SQLite's online backup API snapshot: recent commits may remain in the separate write-ahead log (WAL), outside the main file.
  2. Back up that snapshot, the original media, procedures, config and model lock to an encrypted repository on a second disk.
  3. Make a second encrypted off-site copy.

Only SQLite online backup and read-back were tested; encryption, remote repositories and scheduling remain unrun.

Once a month, restore into a new directory on spare storage and check it:

  1. Open it with the fixed SQLite runtime, run integrity and foreign-key checks, and count customers and invoices.
  2. Recompute balances.
  3. Retrieve a known paint record and its photo.
  4. Compare an expected manifest of accepted event IDs and media hashes.
  5. Practice restoring from the off-site copy, using the recovery secret.

An upload alone does not prove Henderson's colour is recoverable.

Reach it from the truck, privately

A private VPN such as Tailscale reaches the app from the truck. The plan must permit business use; this design does not assume a free personal plan applies.16

  • Bind the application to its private interface and deny public access.
  • Require an app login as well as device access, and revoke lost phones.

Five days in one owner's work

These invented days use the caps and one property per fresh prompt; measured prompts are separate fixtures.

1. Draft the estimate after a site visit

The owner uploads a voice note and four Vale room photos: “Dining walls, two coats; repair the crack; keep existing trim.” The phone carries property ID 2. Local Whisper transcribes; 2B captions photos and flags a partly readable can label. Unaccepted episodes link to originals; the owner corrects a misheard dimension before pricing.

Budget: the Estimate row above (6,900 tokens in total). get_property, recall_notes and read_procedure supply the transcript, captions, surfaces, prior work, pricing rules, template and exclusions. propose_estimate returns surface IDs, measured units and a scope description; the price tool, application code the model cannot call, turns them into line rows and totals.

The owner approves dimensions, prep, coats, product, exclusions and exact lines. Commit the estimate, lines and one event; sending needs separate approval. Later changes require change orders.

2. Find an old colour without guessing

“What colour and sheen did we use in the Hendersons' dining room in 2024?” get_property reads the surface record and recall_notes brings the signed completion-card excerpt. Use the 2024 job despite newer notes. Authorise against today's tenure, then select scoped evidence by job ID or validity at the requested date. Historical evidence never grants present access. The fixture checks different cited answers for 2024 and now.

Budget: the Historic colour row of the budget table. It reads semantic and episodic notes plus the surface record, and writes nothing. The expected answer is “Benjamin Moore HC-172, eggshell, from the dining-room completion card dated 12 June 2024,” with the source ID.

Internal answers need no approval. For conflicting cards and receipts, show both for owner review; vector rank cannot decide truth. Corrections follow the surface-revision transaction above; original job evidence stays.

3. Chase unpaid invoices and unanswered estimates

A morning query lists invoices past due and sent estimates unanswered for the owner-set interval. SQL finds the candidates; a semantic search for “still owes” would not. For Henderson invoice 1, line rows total $1,000, a recorded deposit is $400 and the balance is $600. The fixture returns Vale's estimate sent on 1 November and asserts that Vale has no job, invoice or payment.

Budget per account: the Estimate/invoice chase row. read_ledger supplies amounts and dates, read_procedure chooses the cadence and propose_message drafts only a non-financial note slot, rejected for digits or currency terms. Code renders invoice numbers, dates and balances into the template. Recall can miss disputes, so SQL decides eligibility using the rules above; recall_notes only personalises qualifying accounts.

The owner reviews each recipient, amount and tone. The adapter records send results; failures stay pending. Recheck eligibility before sending; later payments or contact holds suppress reminders.

4. Prepare tomorrow's schedule and materials

“Prepare tomorrow's jobs and what to pick up.” read_schedule finds booked jobs and accepted change orders, then get_property reads access and surface rows. recall_notes adds the recent diary about leftover paint. read_procedure supplies the rounding and pickup checklist.

Budget per job: the Tomorrow row. Make one property-scoped call per job; code concatenates results. Calculate quantities from approved lines and coverage rules, rounding purchase units up before model formatting. The draft separates planned purchases, confirmed stock and uncertain leftovers.

Purchases and schedule changes need approval; listing writes nothing. Accepted material updates write revised requirements and an event. An empty can creates an episode, then a reviewed stock correction. Route optimisation and travel-time promises are excluded; helpers see access data only for assigned jobs.

5. Ask about repaint work at the right time

“Prepare this month's repaint reminders.” The owner assigns surface-specific repaint_on dates; these business choices do not assert model-predicted paint lifetimes. The eligibility query selects dates due, current ownership, marketing consent and no contact hold or dispute, so a sold house never triggers a message to the previous owner.

def api(route, data=None):
    raw = None if data is None else json.dumps(data).encode()

def connect():
    assert sqlite3.sqlite_version == '3.51.3'

def recall(db, customer, prop, query, as_of='2026-11-20'):
    t = time.perf_counter()
    vector = embed(['Instruct: Retrieve painting job history.\nQuery: ' + query])[0]
    eligible = '''n.customer_id=? AND n.property_id=?
        AND n.valid_from<=? AND (n.valid_to IS NULL OR n.valid_to>?)'''
    args = (customer, prop, as_of, as_of)
    # Filters apply before LIMIT in both searches; no global top-k leakage.
    terms = ' OR '.join('"' + w.replace('"', '""') + '"'
                        for w in re.findall(r'[\w-]+', query))
    lexical = db.execute('''SELECT n.id FROM note_fts JOIN notes n
        ON n.id=note_fts.rowid WHERE note_fts MATCH ? AND ''' + eligible +
        ' ORDER BY bm25(note_fts) LIMIT 8', (terms, *args)).fetchall() if terms else []
    dense = db.execute('''SELECT n.id FROM vectors v JOIN notes n
        ON n.id=v.note_id WHERE ''' + eligible +
        ' ORDER BY vec_distance_cosine(v.embedding,?) LIMIT 8',
        (*args, vector)).fetchall()
    scores = {}
    for branch in (lexical, dense):
        for rank, row in enumerate(branch, 1):
            scores[row['id']] = scores.get(row['id'], 0) + 1 / (60 + rank)
    ids = sorted(scores, key=lambda i: (-scores[i], i))[:2]
    rows = [dict(db.execute('SELECT * FROM notes WHERE id=?', (i,)).fetchone())
            for i in ids]
    assert all(r['customer_id'] == customer and r['property_id'] == prop
               for r in rows)
    print('recall:', ids, 'seconds:', round(time.perf_counter() - t, 3))
    return rows

def finish_fallback(db, prop, as_of, evidence, finish):

def draft_errors(text, cents):
    errors = []
    if re.search(r'\[[^]\n]+\]', text):
        errors.append('placeholder')
    if re.search(r'attach(?:ed|ment|ments)?|enclos(?:ed|ure)', text, re.I):
        errors.append('attachment claim')
    dollars = re.findall(r'\$([\d,]+(?:\.\d{1,2})?)', text)
    dollars += re.findall(r'([\d,]+(?:\.\d{1,2})?)\s*dollars?\b', text, re.I)
    cent_values = re.findall(r'([\d,]+)\s*cents?\b', text, re.I)
    from decimal import Decimal
    amounts = [int(Decimal(x.replace(',', '')) * 100) for x in dollars]
    amounts += [int(x.replace(',', '')) for x in cent_values]
    if not amounts or any(x != cents for x in amounts):
        errors.append('amount versus SQL')
    return errors

Deployment must also test disputed-colour review, sold-tenure isolation, withdrawn-consent suppression, provider_ref deduplication and rejection of competing revision writes.

At the planning allowance of 10 to 30 output tokens/s, a 600-token reminder takes 20 to 60 seconds to decode, plus input processing, model loading and retrieval. Prefer templates without personalisation. At 40 seconds each, 100 reminders occupy about 67 minutes before review: serial generation and owner approval limit throughput before database capacity.

Costs cover electricity, depreciation, downloads and owner labour, without hosted token fees. At an assumed 30 W average extra draw for eight hours a day, annual energy is 0.030 × 8 × 365 = 87.6 kWh; multiply by the owner's tariff. This unmeasured Mac power assumption cannot establish maintenance savings against a $17 plan.

Before scaling for customer growth, measure queue wait, prompt length, peak memory, writer wait and retried writes:

  • If two simultaneous staff sessions regularly wait over 30 seconds for a free model slot, try a second inference machine before changing the database.
  • If workers need continuous concurrent writes, or writer wait repeatedly exceeds the five-second timeout, adopt the Postgres and Redis patterns in Chapters 08 and 09: Postgres owns transactions; Redis holds disposable sessions and caches. Chapter 18 explains the wider choice.

Risks and what the owner still owns

Bookkeeping correctness. SQL computes totals, the model drafts and the owner approves. Commit approved rows and events atomically; enforce the request ID/payload retry protocol above (Chapter 17). Reconcile processor settlements, fees, refunds and bank receipts, and export for the accountant. Import customers/properties with distinct tenure IDs, reconcile opening invoices and balances to the old books, and leave imported colours unapproved until source-matched. Keep both systems until a parallel billing period agrees and a restore passes.

Customer privacy. Local inference still needs access controls on the operating system, phone, backups and adapters. Exclude card numbers from this database and third-party health or family details from job notes. Chapter 17 covers deletion and rebuilding derived indexes; an append-only log is no reason to keep personal data forever.

Losing the machine. Independent backups preserve context and books together. Approved notes can rebuild embeddings; summaries cannot reconstruct missing signed completion cards. Restore authoritative data before model caches; keep recovery credentials accessible without the failed machine.

Mistakes in customer-facing prose. A model can sound certain about an unsourced finish, offer a discount or repeat an access code. Show the owner citations and a deterministic money panel, reject unsupported claims, extra recipients and unapproved promises, and fall back to a template or “needs review.”

Professional boundaries. Accountants determine treatment, tax rules and reconciliation; processors or banks move and verify money. Local models only assemble evidence and explanations. Start with sourced colour answers and correct reviewed estimates, then migrate records under control.
