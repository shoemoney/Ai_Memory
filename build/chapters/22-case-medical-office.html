A doctor's office

Replace contact, billing, scheduling and phone interfaces with local agents that remember only what each task needs.

Maturity

Reference design; isolated text smoke test

3 clinicians, 10 staff, 4,000 patients

Capacity target

Open weights; practice-controlled hardware

The practice and the boundary

Phone calls, reminders, insurance cards and billing questions occupy much of a doctor's office day.

This engineering design is not legal, compliance, tax or clinical advice; the practice owns risk analysis. Regulatory references are US federal; elsewhere, remap the legal gates. People and events are invented.

Meadow Lane Primary Care has three clinicians, ten staff and about four thousand patients. Agents handle contact, scheduling, billing support and phones; clinical questions and urgent requests go to a human. The phone system has a hard-coded emergency path outside the language model.

Full deployment requires engineering maintenance capacity; otherwise retain existing interfaces or adopt SQL memory/gates without phones.

All models, including speech, vision and embeddings, use practice-controlled hardware. Block inference-server outbound traffic; copy weights in. No patient data reaches providers through inference, telemetry, crash uploads or remote support.

Replace four interfaces: patient-contact customer relationship management (CRM), billing support, scheduling and automated attendant. The practice-management and electronic health record (EHR) system remains authoritative for clinical records and the ledger. OpenEMR offers records, practice management, scheduling and electronic billing, but is not required. Certification and security depend on installation.1

That system owns demographics, appointments, encounters and ledger postings. PostgreSQL holds administrative memory and versioned read models. An adapter imports authoritative changes with source IDs and revision numbers. The scheduler’s transaction API prevents conflicting reservations through the LLM screen; this design supplies authorization and context assembly.

Staff still approve codes, correct charges and release claims; the adapter handles eligibility inquiries and responses, claims and remittances without the LLM. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) lists adopted electronic transaction standards, including X12 5010: 270/271 for eligibility, 837 for claims and 835 for payment and remittance. Billing reads posted rows; models never invent claims or interpret insurance contracts from memory.2

The American Medical Association (AMA) requires permission or licensing for Current Procedural Terminology (CPT). Keep licensed tables and versions in billing; never train on or distribute the code set with this example.3

Local models do not make the office local-only

External services include the carrier or Session Initiation Protocol (SIP) trunk, emergency calling, claims clearinghouse, payer networks, card payments, e-prescribing and messaging providers. Allowlist each destination at its adapter. Send the agent a payment token, never card details. E-prescribing stays entirely in the clinical workflow.

The US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) requires written assurances from business associates, outside parties providing covered functions/services involving protected health information (PHI). Assess flows and obtain applicable business associate agreements for clearinghouses, messaging and PHI support. Payment and payer classifications differ.4

The conduit exception excludes transmission-only services with transient storage from business-associate status; voicemail and transcript stores retain PHI and fall outside it. An outside encrypted-PHI backup custodian remains a business associate without the key, requiring contract and risk review. This design uses a second practice-controlled appliance off-site.5

Architecture decisions, November 2026

The two language-model lanes

Main: Qwen3.8-27B, a dense Apache-2.0 native vision-language model (VLM), 27B parameters, 262,144 native context tokens; operational cap 16,384. Its card documents tools and per-request non-thinking mode. Use it for drafts, reconciled balance explanations, summaries, background note-taking and card extraction. The four-bit Q4_K_M GGUF size estimates weight memory only; production uses a different vLLM artifact, and the smoke test uses Ollama. Pin the serving artifact, hash and quantization in the release manifest.6

Phone: Apache-2.0 Qwen3.5-9B, with vision, documented tools and 262,144 native context tokens; use Q4-class at 8,192. Chapter 06’s Ollama llama.cpp Q4_K_M (4-bit GGUF) has 5.6 GB weights, 6.6 GB with projector, within the 7 GB allowance. Its size reserves speech capacity and shortens prefill (prompt processing before output). Main-lane failure pauses drafts and card extraction; staff use existing interfaces. Reserve the phone lane until mixed-load replay passes; its fallback is a deterministic menu and human transfer, never an untested model.7

Both cards document thinking on by default. For administrative turns, the gateway sends chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking=false on every request (Chapter 06). Verify that no hidden reasoning consumes the output budget.

Use one continuously batched serving lane per GPU with separate admission queues. Serve with vLLM on a pinned, supported native 4-bit artifact, such as AWQ if supported for the exact model and cards; otherwise re-budget a higher-precision build. Re-measure either choice; GGUF sizes are planning proxies. Constrain tool arguments and extraction results with a JSON schema, then validate patient ID, field types, policy version and authority in application code. vLLM supports structured output constraints; the model can still select the wrong action.8

At four calls, target useful text within 350 ms and about 50 output tokens/s per stream. Measure complete tool calls separately; tool tokens and filler are not spoken answers. Four 2,500-token prefills in 350 ms need about 29,000 prompt tokens/s uncached: put stable policy first, enable prefix caching and measure cold and cached turns separately. Missed targets reduce admission or trigger transfer, never clinical small talk.

Three options are rejected:

  • A 120B model for the phone loop, because its larger allocation and prefill compete with speech for a task that usually selects one tool.
  • A single 64 GB Apple-silicon Mac: both lanes and speech share memory and one accelerator, so card images can delay speech. A one-call office could validate it.
  • Hosted inference, which violates the premise.

The memory the model may use

In Chapter 00’s terms, semantic memory holds approved administrative knowledge; episodic memory, dated contacts; procedural memory, approved policy; working memory, the current task. These need no separate products. SQL holds semantic and episodic records plus the release hash of each approved policy file; the gateway assembles working memory, keeping live call state in Redis. Model weights are never updated from patient conversations.

Records first, prose second

PostgreSQL keeps patient/external IDs, coverage history, appointment, billing-encounter, charge and claim references, remittance postings, payments, communications and call records. Use views or imported read models stamped with their imported source revision (the adapter watermark); never create a competing ledger. Clinical notes, diagnoses and treatment decisions stay in the clinical system. Billing encounters expose service dates and ledger IDs without progress notes.

Coverage uses Chapter 14’s valid time (when the plan covered the patient) and recorded time (when the office believed it). Corrections atomically close the old recorded interval and insert its replacement under an exclusion constraint, preserving earlier answers. As-of tools take both dates and show source and freshness.

Approve contact method/times, language, interpreter need, transport/mobility, billing arrangements and representative authority. Record source, author, valid/recorded intervals, reviewer and sensitivity class. Representative scope needs expiry and documentary evidence; “My sister can help” requests review, not balance disclosure.

Episodes hold message threads, front-desk notes, call turns and summaries linked to original communication IDs. Summaries index rather than replace evidence. Preserve speaker, time and low speech confidence. Clinical material arriving in voicemail goes to a restricted handoff queue outside administrative retrieval; the agent can say it passed a message to staff but cannot interpret it.

Versioned, approved procedural files specify scheduling type/duration, permitted provider/location, preparation and override authority; phone disclosure, identity checks and emergency wording. Keep escalation, reminder consent and opt-out in separate dated files. Releases record approver and hash; patient text cannot edit policy.

Before rank fusion/reranking, scope full-text/vector searches (Chapter 05) by patient, authenticated actor, allowlisted task/classes, signed verification and policy version. Row-level security (Chapter 08) adds a database boundary. Vector matches grant no authority; phone searches never cross patients.

Use task-specific field allowlists. HHS’s purpose-appropriate limits include exceptions for treatment and disclosures to individuals. This engineering policy applies task limits even under exceptions; it does not state the whole law.14

Who can write

Only the practice system writes bookings, coverage and financial postings; adapters import them. Staff approve/correct facts, billing adjustments, representative permissions and insurance corrections. Main-model background note-taking proposes quotes with communication IDs; it cannot write approved facts.

Nightly deduplication flags conflicts, preserving sources, identities and approval requirements. Staff review old/proposed values, source and effective date; approval atomically commits version/audit. Chapter 17’s action IDs/outbox cover embeddings and cache invalidation; changed-payload retries fail.

Default raw audio off; obtain state retention advice. Authorized, consented debug audio expires after 7 days, transcript copies after 30 days, proposals after 14 days. Counsel-approved schedules/holds govern approved facts, retained communications, financial records, consent and audits. Delete vectors, indexes, caches, exports and transcript derivatives too; retain tombstones against backup resurrection.

Backup, restore and emergency mode

Back up schema, grants, source versions, approved policies, deletion markers and adapter watermark together; export roles separately under administrative protection. Encrypt local/off-site point-in-time backups; authorized staff hold keys separately. Unmeasured targets: at most 15 minutes lost and 30-minute human-only recovery.

Quarterly, stop model access, restore an isolated database, reapply deletions/access changes and test patient/sensitivity denials before reconnecting. In emergency mode, staff use the existing EHR scheduler and ledger directly, and the PBX plays its fixed scripts. Record authoritative changes; staff reconcile paper downtime logs before re-enabling agents. LLMs never merge downtime notes into bookings.

Access, audit and the phone boundary

Identify staff and automated services individually; bind actions to service principal and requesting staff/verified caller. Apply role changes promptly. Use five-minute console logoff, short-lived signed verification and privileged-change reauthentication. Under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Security Rule (45 CFR 164.312), individual logins, actor authentication and per-recall audits support identification, authentication and audit requirements; assess the deployed system against the rule. Inactivity logoff addresses (a)(2)(iii); encrypted disks protect stored PHI under (a)(2)(iv). Assess transmission encryption separately under (e)(2)(ii), including audio-worker and database links. Addressable does not mean optional: assess each such specification, implement it if reasonable and appropriate, or document why not and implement a reasonable equivalent alternative where appropriate. The audit fields are a design choice, not regulatory text.15

Segment PBX voice, managed staff clients and protected inference/memory servers. Permit PBX access only to the server-segment audio worker; carrier and staff-phone paths use separate rules. Models cannot reach databases, carriers or internet directly; only the gateway recalls context and dispatches tools. Encrypt inter-segment and gateway/database/audio-worker/adapter links. Bind AudioSocket to loopback, tunnel inter-host traffic with mutual authentication/encryption and block direct cross-segment AudioSocket. Separate database administration’s management path.

Keep per-actor credentials in the trusted API. One service account loses session_user identity; SECURITY DEFINER masks SET ROLE’s current_user. Pooled-role policies need reviewed SECURITY INVOKER and table grants, never untrusted custom-setting identity. Sync staff scope from EHR access. Four phone logins, one per admitted slot, start without patient grants. Verification grants subject/task briefly; check expiry every recall, revoke at hang-up, clear before reuse and sweep. Expiry must deny after worker crashes without cleanup; Redis cannot revoke SQL grants.

Audit every read, including cache hits: actor, patient, task, request ID, policy version, query scope, source IDs/versions, returned fields and exact-context digest. Writes record before/after versions and approver. A protected context archive reconstructs answers without PHI in ordinary logs. Send denials and infrastructure errors to a separate security sink; staff review unusual volume and cross-patient denials daily.

Caller ID is a lookup hint, never authentication. The deterministic verifier collects numeric identifiers by keypad; speech requires exact matching above an approved confidence floor. Transfer on low confidence or a second miss. Changes needing stronger assurance require a callback or secure portal challenge. Failed or ambiguous verification, household numbers and withheld caller ID all transfer to staff without revealing whether a record exists. The model receives only a signed verification result and cannot set it to true. Check recorded representative authority.

HHS permits appointment messages but advises limiting what is disclosed and accommodating reasonable confidential-communication requests. The default voicemail is the practice's approved name and callback number, with no patient name, balance, procedure, test or diagnosis. If even the practice name would violate a recorded request, suppress the call and route to staff.16

Recording consent and outbound reminders

Audio recording and transcription need separate consent choices. Persist neither before approved notice and affirmative choice; declining callers reach staff. Consent policy documents local processing, storage, retention and downstream uses. Counsel determines applicable recording law; area code is insufficient. No state rule is asserted here.

The retrieved current text of 47 CFR 64.1200 includes a healthcare wireless exemption with these conditions:20

  • The patient provided the number.
  • The message identifies the provider.
  • It serves a listed healthcare purpose, with no financial or marketing content.
  • Keep messages short, with at most one daily and three combined weekly voice or text messages.
  • Opt-out is easy, and the request is honored immediately.

The exemption also requires no charge, and the residential healthcare provision has its own daily and weekly limits.

Require recorded opt-in and counsel-approved scripts. Apply the combined-channel cap and immediate opt-out across all queues, exclude billing content and offer key/voice opt-out plus approved voicemail callback. Test memory/consent gates with outbound AI voice disabled. HIPAA permission does not establish TCPA permission.

Five tasks, with the memories exposed

1. An inbound reschedule from a known number

Invented patient Mira Vale calls from the number on file. The lookup yields a candidate, not an authorized context, so the verifier establishes identity first. The gateway then reads language and contact preferences, the current appointment reference, and visit-duration and provider rules. Redis holds the live request “move to Tuesday afternoon”, not a guessed replacement.

The scheduler returns valid slots; Mira selects one and hears its date and time. Her confirmation authorizes an ordinary move under deterministic rules and an atomic scheduler check. A conflict prompts fresh slots; success returns a receipt. Only exceptions need front-desk approval. After hang-up, save the permitted communication and proposed summary; reviewers approve enduring preferences.

2. A new patient and an insurance-card photo

Unverified invented caller Tomas Reed transfers to front-desk intake. Staff collect restricted provisional contacts without searching other patients’ episodes. With consent, send a single-use practice-hosted upload link through an assessed provider. The main VLM proposes payer, member ID, subscriber relationship and visible dates; unreadable fields are null.

Staff compare photo/fields, resolve duplicate identity and approve intake. The eligibility adapter checks benefits; cards cannot prove coverage. Approval writes sourced coverage versions/EHR reference and releases permitted facts. Approve interpreter need as semantic memory; symptoms go to staff outside administrative memory.

3. “Why does this patient still owe a balance?”

An authorized biller asks about invented patient Inez North. For the balance task, the gateway reads posted charges, adjustments, insurer payments, remittance reasons and patient payments from the authoritative ledger, plus approved financial arrangements. The model gets a dated, sourced breakdown, not a conversational estimate.

For an invented ledger example, a $180 charge, an $80 contractual adjustment and a $60 insurer payment leave $40 outstanding. The posted remittance assigns that $40 to the patient, and a later $10 patient payment leaves $30 due. Deterministic code does the arithmetic; the model explains it and cites those five rows. A pending appeal or unmatched remittance is shown as unresolved. Staff approve any correction, refund, adjustment or outgoing balance letter. The explanation can be an episodic communication, never a timeless semantic fact that the patient “owes $30”.

4. Tomorrow's reminders and waitlist backfill

Read tomorrow’s appointments/policy; recall only contact preferences, consent, suppression and language. Atomically reserve each patient’s combined-channel daily/weekly allowance before queueing; opt-out cancels queued work and retries.

A cancellation triggers the waitlist tool, which selects only staff-approved nonclinical eligibility rules and a patient who opted into short-notice contact. The agent may offer the slot but never silently moves an appointment: reserve a short hold, collect confirmation, then book atomically. Staff resolve clinical priority or special access needs. Write communication status, opt-out or consent receipts, and appointment receipts. Never recall financial arrangements to persuade someone to accept a slot.

5. All contact during the last year

An authorized supervisor requests invented patient Lena Brook’s contact year. Return all authorized call summaries, messages and front-desk notes in that interval, paginated with completeness count. Exclude clinical records; withhold restricted contacts without extra grant and disclose that omission.

Return a deterministic paginated timeline, one entry per authorized source ID, reconciled to the authorized count. Separately summarize chronological batches with source IDs; a final model pass produces a synopsis and flags conflicting dates. Staff open sources and approve saved summaries: versioned derived episodes with exact range and source-set digest, preserving source messages. Context caps never justify silently omitting nine months; the synopsis is not exhaustive.

The small memory schema

This memory/audit fixture covers isolation, temporal coverage and sourced facts, communications and call metadata. It proves a small recall boundary and local context use. Financial tables and appointment transactions remain authoritative elsewhere, linked by immutable external IDs.

Staff logins lack direct SELECT. Non-login, non-superuser recall_owner neither owns tables nor has BYPASSRLS, so enabled policies apply. FORCE binds non-superuser owners, not postgres. SECURITY DEFINER preserves authenticated session_user for patient/staff/task/class policies. Audits store actor, patient, task, operation and returned JSON. Before deployment, move postgres-owned allowed/class_allowed helpers to recall_owner, grant scope-table reads and revoke PUBLIC execution.

The fixture omits the production grant-expiry checks described under Access, audit and the phone boundary.

Synthetic three-dimensional vectors and full-text queries test patient, task and class isolation. Tied ranks place authorized patients inside LIMIT, so the test cannot prove filtering before LIMIT; add an authorized patient beyond that boundary. Production uses 1,024 dimensions and patient-scoped hybrid search (Chapter 05). Out-of-scope patients or tasks return empty arrays and an audit row, indistinguishable from no data; production must explicitly deny and audit. Callers controlling the transaction can roll back a recall receipt. The production API must commit before releasing context and send failed reads and transport errors to the independent event sink.

CREATE FUNCTION recall(p int, job text, asof date, known timestamptz)
RETURNS jsonb LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY DEFINER

DECLARE result jsonb; live_now timestamptz := clock_timestamp();
BEGIN
  PERFORM set_config('app.patient',p::text,true);
  PERFORM set_config('app.task',job,true);
  SELECT jsonb_build_object(
    'coverage',coalesce((SELECT jsonb_agg(jsonb_build_object(
      'id',id,'payer',payer)) FROM public.coverage
      WHERE valid @> asof AND recorded @> known),'[]'::jsonb),

      'id',id,'body',body)) FROM public.fact

    VALUES(session_user,p,job,'recall',result);
  RETURN result;
END $$;

CREATE FUNCTION search_facts(p int, job text, asof date, known timestamptz,
                            query text, query_vector vector(3))
RETURNS jsonb LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY DEFINER
SET search_path=pg_catalog,public AS $$
DECLARE result jsonb;
BEGIN
  PERFORM set_config('app.patient',p::text,true);
  PERFORM set_config('app.task',job,true);
  SELECT jsonb_build_object(
    'vector',coalesce((SELECT jsonb_agg(id ORDER BY distance,id) FROM (
      SELECT id,embedding <=> query_vector AS distance FROM public.fact
      WHERE valid @> asof AND recorded @> known AND embedding IS NOT NULL
      ORDER BY distance,id LIMIT 12) v),'[]'::jsonb),
    'keyword',coalesce((SELECT jsonb_agg(id ORDER BY rank DESC,id) FROM (
      SELECT id,ts_rank(lex,plainto_tsquery('english',query)) AS rank
      FROM public.fact WHERE valid @> asof AND recorded @> known
        AND lex @@ plainto_tsquery('english',query)
      ORDER BY rank DESC,id LIMIT 12) k),'[]'::jsonb)) INTO result;
  INSERT INTO public.audit(actor,patient_id,task,operation,returned)
    VALUES(session_user,p,job,'search',result);
  RETURN result;
END $$;

Scope and scale

Four simultaneous three-minute calls at the typical turn size create 2,560 output tokens over 180 seconds (about 14.2 per second) and 80,000 input tokens (444 per second). This prices templated tool-result speech; model-phrased results add a request with separately counted serialized input and output. Cost uses model calls and tokens at the planning rates above; prefill, speech, tool time and queueing add to each figure.

Fact writes, approvals and exact reads need no generation unless an explanation is requested. Embedding one approved 512-token chunk adds one embedding call and about 4 KB of raw vector storage, before source text, versions and audit. A nightly fact proposal adds one bounded note-taking call per communication batch.

Worked example, not a budget: $12,000 hardware over 36 months, $50 monthly energy and 2,000 calls a month gives ($333.33 + $50) / 2,000 = about $0.19 per call. Substitute a vendor quote and metered or tariffed energy; recalculate at half and double volume. Carrier fees, support, backup, staff review, shared back-office use, application host, standby, off-site appliance, PBX and engineering cost extra.

At 4,000 patients with 100 administrative chunks each, 400,000 × 1,024 × 4 bytes is about 1.64 GB of raw vector payload, before text, indexes, audit and replicas. Exact patient-scoped search often examines only hundreds of chunks, so start without an approximate index. Transcripts can outgrow facts; prioritize retention and pagination over another vector product. Chapter 08 covers indexing; Chapter 11 is the later option when measured vector workloads justify separation.

Bursts will likely hit inference queueing first, then speech contention. Measure SQL and Redis too: synchronous audit latency can bottleneck recall, and a stalled durable turn writer must halt the session instead of dropping evidence. Watch first-token p95, end-of-turn to first-audio p95, STT realtime factor, missed interruptions, queue depth and recall/audit p95.

Add inference capacity after persistent four-call misses of 1,100 ms despite bounded prompts/reserved queues. Isolate speech on a GPU when STT/TTS misses realtime despite healthy model queues. Expand workers/reads only with passing idempotency/audit tests. Multiple sites require site grants, explicit shared-patient authority, local PBX emergency modes and tested inter-site failures, preserving patient filters. Follow Chapter 18’s scale path and Chapter 17’s receipts.

Risks and release limits

Wrong or stale memory changes calls and displayed plans. Every answer needs source IDs, valid dates and adapter watermark. Suppress expired preferences, flag conflicts and prevent summaries overriding posted appointments/remittances. Test corrections and late insurance changes separately.

Test retrieval, caches, shared server context and handoffs with two patients’ distinct markers, reusing one staff session and model worker to expose carryover. Require database denial and inspect prompts as well as refusals.

Voicemail and patient messages can carry instructions to reveal another record or rewrite office rules (Chapter 17 covers injection through stored memory). Store them as untrusted episodes, delimit them at recall and keep only fields the task allows. Never execute embedded URLs, tool syntax or “approved” statements; approval receipts and row-level security must still reject the action.

A question about a symptom, medication, test result, priority or safety goes to a person, even when the request sounds administrative. Use fixed escalation phrases, and test paraphrases, interruptions and language changes. A clinical-content detector can add a handoff signal but cannot authorize the model to triage. Never claim a medical outcome or safe waiting.

Preserve access to people during clinic-hour outages. Rehearse carrier failure, power loss, lost keys and staffing. HHS's current summary still distinguishes the existing Security Rule from proposed modifications, and this chapter does not rely on secondary reports of future rule deadlines.23

Release needs five signed items; this smoke test supports one boundary, not compliance certification.

  1. Counsel reviews the risk analysis, vendor relationships and agreements, recording and transcription choices, outbound-call authority (including carrier and AI-voice rules), representative access, confidential communication requests, retention and holds, incident response and state-specific duties. Evidence: written review.
  2. Clinical leaders approve handoff wording and staffing. Evidence: signed scripts.
  3. Engineers verify artifact hashes and caller verification. Evidence: hash report and a verifier test report showing that a verified caller gets only the bound subject's context and that failed, ambiguous, expired and out-of-scope cases yield none, including a crashed worker followed by another pickup.
  4. Engineers replay the five task flows, barge-in, transfer and prompt leakage tests. Gates: zero cross-patient marker disclosures, zero bookings without a receipt, every emergency and clinical paraphrase transferred, and the 1,100 ms budget met at p95 with four calls. Tunnel loss must transfer without plaintext fallback. Evidence: replay results.
  5. Engineers run restore and failure drills, including denied restored reads and role recovery. Blocking write test: overlapping correction must fail under the exclusion constraint while a concurrent interpreter fact succeeds. Evidence: drill logs and constraint-test output.
